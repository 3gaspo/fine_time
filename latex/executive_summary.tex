\section*{Research question}
Fine TIME asks whether task-specific fine-tuning improves pretrained
time-series foundation models over their frozen zero-shot forecasts. Each
task compares the same backbone before and after adaptation, using the same
forecast windows and evaluation support.

\section*{Implemented experiment}
The first experiment covers Chronos-2, Chronos-Bolt, and TS-ICL on the
90-task Selectime subset used by the project. Each task produces paired
frozen and fine-tuned forecasts. Fine-tuning uses only model-eligible
training windows with finite input and output support; it does not substitute
an unfitted model when no such window exists. Seasonal Naive supplies the
shared reference grid and scaling contract.

\section*{Observed evidence}
The first synchronized run completed 90 frozen/fine-tuned task pairs for each
backbone. Lower MASE is better.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Model & Frozen & Fine-tuned & Change & Win rate \\
\midrule
Chronos-2 & 1.06923 & 1.05234 & $-1.58\%$ & $75.56\%$ \\
Chronos-Bolt & 1.16790 & 1.12307 & $-3.84\%$ & $80.00\%$ \\
TS-ICL (batch 8) & 1.09871 & 1.09506 & $-0.33\%$ & $81.11\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Fine-tuning improved the equal-task-weighted mean in all three observed arms,
most strongly for Chronos-Bolt. The task-level effect was heterogeneous, so
these averages do not imply a benefit on every task.

\section*{Current boundary}
The Chronos-2 and Chronos-Bolt comparisons remain compatible with the current
configuration. The observed TS-ICL arm used the former batch size of 8; the
current default is 32. Its 90 fine-tuned checkpoints and adapted evaluations
must therefore be rerun before a current TS-ICL conclusion is available.
Frozen TS-ICL evaluations remain reusable. The existing report also omits the
exact selected input-manifest references; that provenance repair requires only
report regeneration. Results use one seed and 100 optimization steps, so they
do not establish statistical significance or broader-profile generalization.
